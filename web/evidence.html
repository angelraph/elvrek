Honest result. The trusted check got much better after tuning, but on real arrest ECG it still does not meet the American Heart Association performance goals. Until it does, the 0.8 s step should be read as a timed pause with a careful check, not a diagnosis.

57 records from two public PhysioNet databases. The check measures five things on 0.8 s of clean ECG, including how much of the signal sits in the 3 to 9 Hz fibrillation band. Its thresholds were tuned on CUDB only, with a rule fixed in advance (leave at least 95% of every non-shockable rhythm alone, then find as much VF as possible), and then scored once on VFDB, which the tuning never saw. Longer looks of 1.6 and 2.4 s were tested and did not help enough to justify the extra hands-off time.
