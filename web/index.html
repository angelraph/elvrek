Why it matters

The pause before a shock costs lives

In cardiac arrest, every extra five seconds of pause before a shock has been linked to lower odds of survival.1 Mechanical CPR keeps compressions going, but the compressions, the breaths and the defibrillator usually run on separate clocks. Elvrek puts them on one.

Drag to turn the arm. The piston keeps time at 110 compressions a minute.

One brain, three jobs

Compress. Breathe. Shock.

One real-time process owns the piston, the timing of each breath and the shock line. Everything else may only make suggestions.

Steady compressions

100 to 120 a minute, 5 to 6 cm deep, full recoil every time, with a hard force ceiling and depth that adapts to a stiff chest.
